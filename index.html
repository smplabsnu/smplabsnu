Nair Research Lab

Welcome to the Nair Research Lab

Our lab operates at the dynamic intersection of theoretical physics, advanced interfacial phenomena, and specialized hardware electronics design.

We are dedicated to unravelling complex molecular interactions at boundaries, probing the limits of statistical mechanics, and engineering the custom instrumentation necessary to observe what was previously unmeasurable.

Research Themes

Investigating the boundaries of matter, math, and measurement.

Nanoparticle Interfaces

This core research thrust focuses on the behavior of functionalized iron nanoparticles at liquid-liquid interfaces.

Specifically, we are investigating the complex stabilization mechanics driven by POSS-NH2+ (Polyhedral Oligomeric Silsesquioxanes) functionalization when interacting with ITO (Indium Tin Oxide) glass surfaces. Understanding these interactions is critical for developing next-generation active coatings, advanced sensing applications, and smart materials that respond dynamically to environmental stimuli.

Theoretical Physics & Statistical Mechanics

Our theoretical work provides the foundational models necessary to interpret complex experimental results.

We delve deeply into time-dependent harmonic perturbation and its implications for quantum system transitions. Furthermore, our statistical mechanics research explores non-equilibrium dynamics and phase transitions in confined geometries, applying rigorous mathematical frameworks to predict macroscopic behaviors from microscopic interactions.

Hardware Electronics Design

To measure phenomena at the absolute limits of physical detection, commercial equipment is often insufficient.

We design, simulate, and fabricate bespoke lab instrumentation. A major focus is on low-noise operational amplifier (op-amp) circuitry tailored for highly sensitive signal extraction from our nanoparticle interfaces. By building custom electronics, we eliminate bottlenecks between the physical phenomena and our data acquisition systems.
